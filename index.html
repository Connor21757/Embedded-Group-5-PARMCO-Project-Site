PARMCO

Phone App RP4 Motor Control

An Android app talks over Bluetooth to a Raspberry Pi 4. The Pi drives a Nema17 stepper motor, reads a magnetic encoder and an IR sensor, and reports speed and angle back to the phone. A two-blade propeller on the shaft, read against a printed protractor, shows what the motor is really doing.

Drag the red reference blade. The motor can only stop on a multiple of 0.9°, so a 137° target lands on 136.8°.

What it does

The phone sends short text commands: start and stop, direction, faster and slower, a desired RPM from 10 to 100, calibrate zero, set to zero, an angle target, and logging on and off. The Pi answers about seven times a second with the IR RPM, the encoder RPM, the encoder angle and the calibration state, and writes a 100 Hz CSV log on request.

Two controls give the project its character. Calibrate zero ties the encoder’s zero to 0° on the protractor. Set to zero and angle target then use the encoder to bring the reference blade to an angle, and the Pi switches the motor off once the blade is within one half step.

Maintain mode, which holds a desired RPM using sensor feedback, is not part of Checkpoint 3.
