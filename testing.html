Testing

Every claim the system makes is checked against something that works on a different principle. The encoder agreeing with itself proves nothing; the encoder agreeing with a protractor or a blade count does.

The seven tests

TestWhat it showsIndependent checkShown at
A. RPM sensing and sweepIR, encoder and app RPM agree with the commanded speed from 10 to 100 RPMBlade count or slow-motion videoCP2, CP3, final
B. Open-loop missed stepsWithout feedback the motor loses steps, and the step count no longer matches the shaftProtractor and encoder angleCP3, final
C. Static angle accuracyAngle targets land within one step from both directionsPrinted protractorFinal
D. Step responseHow quickly and cleanly the blade moves from 0° to 90°Protractor and slow-motion videoFinal
E. Disturbance rejectionMaintain mode recovers from a push at slow RPMBlade count or videoFinal
F. Feedback holdCoils stay off at rest and the ±1° band is restored after nudgesProtractor and supply currentFinal
G. Forced desync and return to zeroSet to zero recovers after lost stepsPrinted protractorFinal

Checkpoint 3

Checkpoint 3 covers Test A through the app and Test B from the app in Manual mode with the over-range unlock on. For Test B the software logs the step-count angle (step_angle_deg) next to the real encoder angle (enc_cum_deg), so the moment the two separate marks the loss of sync, and the protractor confirms where the blade actually stopped.

Results for Tests A and B will be posted on this page with their raw logs and the measurement limits of the protractor and the IR sensor.

How data is collected

  1. Set the mode, speed or target for the test.
  2. Start logging from the app and check that the indicator shows the log file name and a running clock.
  3. Run the test while taking an independent reading: a blade count, a slow-motion video or a protractor photo.
  4. Stop logging, copy the file off the Pi, and record the file name on the run sheet.
